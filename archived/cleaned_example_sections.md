## Grammar 2

### The use of *-ēw* "one" to specify

There is no indefinite article in Pahlavi, but the numeral !- \<-1\>
*-ēw* "one" can be added to a noun to signify "one particular, a
certain" and similar. Sometimes it comes close in function to the
English indefinite article, but one should not make it a habit to
translate every English indefinite article by Pahlavi *-ēw*! E.g.:

*rōd-ēw* !WTwL "a (certain) river" *gyāg-ēw būd* WTWB !WkAwïD "there was
a (special) place (where)"

*kanīzag-ēw* !WKcDWK "a (certain) girl" *čiyōn mard-ēw ī* Y 1AWvY WwSC
"like a man who"

### Singular and plural

Normally, the verb agrees with the subject in number.

When plurality is not emphasized, but rather species, the singular is
normally used, e.g.:

*pad āsmān stārag ud axtar ud abāxtar ast*

"there are \[*ast*: see below\] stars, constellations, and planets in
the sky" (not *hēnd*)

When two or more 3rd plural past tense forms follow, the plural may be
indicated on the last only, e.g.:

*pad kerbag kerdan tuxšāg būd*

*ud az wināh pahrēxtār būd hēnd*

"they were (*būd*) diligent (*tuxšāg*) in performing (*kerdan*
\[infinitive\]) good deeds

and kept away (*pahrēxtār būd hēnd*) from sin (*az* *wināh*)"

The noun *mardōm* "people, humans" is always plural:

*ēn mardōm kadām hēnd*

"which (= who) are these people?"

####  The plural ending *-ān* with plural nouns governed by prepositions.

The plural ending *-ān* is used when nouns are governed by prepositions
(postpositions), e.g.:

*ēn kanīg ō abārīgān kanīgān nē homānāg*

"this young woman is not like (other) young women"

*Zarduxšt pad mardōmān rād ud rāst būd*

"Zarduxšt was generous and straight/truthful toward people"

More examples of the ending *-ān* are given below. See also Lesson 11.

### 

### The adjective/adverb *was* "much, many, very"

This adjective is very commonly used to qualify nouns and adjectives,
e.g.,

*andar ērān šahr was mazdēsn būd hēnd*

"in the land of the Iranians (= Iran), there were many Mazdayasnians"

*ēn kanīzag was hučihr ān rēdag was huwīr*

"this girl is very pretty, that boy is very manly"

Note the alternative word order, in which *was* is the predicate of the
noun it qualifies:

*andar ērān šahr was mowmard hēnd*

"in the land of the Iranians, there are many Zoroastrian priests"

beside

*andar ērān šahr mowmard was hēnd*

"in the land of the Iranians, Zoroastrian priests are many"

### Possession 1

Possession can be expressed by simply putting the possessor in front of
what is possessed, similar to English *In God's name* (as opposed to *In
the name of God*).

If the possessor is a plural noun, it usually [takes the ending
*-ān*]{.underline}, e.g.:

*pad Ohrmazd nām* "in Ohrmazd's name"

*pad yazdān nām* "in the gods' name"

*dēwān dām* "the creation of the evil gods."

*andar mardān ruwānān* "in men's souls"

###  "To be" and "not to be"

To express existence, the verbs *ast* "exists" and *nēst* "does not
exist" are used, past tense *būd*, future *bawēd* "there will be.

These forms are also used to express "there is, there are."

The plural forms *hēnd*, *nē hēnd*, *būd hēnd* are used when plurality
is emphasized.

Examples:

*wahišt ast ud dōšox ast*

"Paradise exists, and Hell exists; there is a Paradise and there is a
Hell"

*Ahrimen andar gētīy nēst*

"Ahrimen does not exist in this world"

*andar wahišt bazzakkar nēst*

"there is no evil-doer in Paradise, there are no evil-doers in Paradise"

*andar dōšox kerbakkar nē hēnd*

"there are no doers of good deeds in Hell"

*andar dōšox yazd nē būd hēnd*

"there have been no gods (ever) in hell, there have never been any \..."

###  "To have"

The verbs "to be" and "not to be" are also used to express
possession. Grammatically, we could say that the possessor
is indirect object. Plural nouns take the ending *-ān*,
e.g.:

*amāh ēč nēst*

"we have nothing" ("there is nothing for us")

*murwān* *parr ast*

"birds have wing(s)" ("for the birds there are wings")

## Grammar 3

### Adjectives

Adjectives can qualify a noun directly or as predicate of "to be" (many
examples in lessons 1-2).

Adjectives can be placed before the noun they qualify, as in English,
e.g.:

*pad zarrēn gāh* *pad wazurg šādīh*

"on a golden throne, on a throne of gold" "in great joy"

More commonly adjectives follow the noun, being attached to it by [the
particle *ī*]{.underline} ( Y \<Y\>), often referred to as the *izafe*,
e.g.:

*wistarg ī xūb* *Rašn ī rāst*

"a good carpet" "the straight Rašn" (a god of judgment)

*kanīzag ī was hučihr ud rēdag ī was huwīr*

"a very pretty girl, a very manly boy"

The particle can also be used after the "specifying" *-ēw*, e.g.:

*rōd-ēw ī wazurg*

"a (certain) big river"

It can also be placed after an intervening verb, e.g.:

*andar mān ātaxš-ēw būd ī wazurg*

"in the house, there was a great fire"

The particle is also used with appositions and similar expressions such
as:

*Ohrmazd ī xwadāy* *kōf ī Harburz*

"Ohrmazd, the Lord" "the mountain Harburz, Mount Harburz"

*kerbag ī gētīy*

"the good deeds (performed) in this world"

Several adjectives with one noun:

*kanīg ī nēk ī was amāwand ī hu-rust ī āzād*

"a beautiful woman, very powerful and well-grown (of tall stature),
noble"

With appositions:

*šahr ī wazurg ī Bābēl* *Ohrmazd ī xwadāy*

"the great land (of) Babylon" "Ohrmazd the lord"

The particle is also used before prepositional phrases:

*nēkīh ī pad wahišt ud anāgīh ī pad dōšox*

"the goodness in Paradise and the evil in Hell" (*AWN*.5.7)

*kanīg-ēw ī ō kanīgān nē homānāg*

"a young woman not like (other) young women" (*MX*.1.167)

##### *ān ī* ( Y ã \<ZK Y\>):

The Pahlavi expression for "the X one," is *ān ī X*
literally, "that X one." This expression can also be used to attach
adjectives to nouns, e.g.:

*ān ī pahlom axwān* "the best existence" = *pahlom axwān* = *axwān ī
pahlom*

*ān ī wattar axwān* "the worst existence" = *wattar axwān* = *axwān ī
wattar*

*ān ī meh kerbag ud ān ī keh* "the greater good deed and the smaller
one"

[The construction with *ān ī* is very common and important, so learn it
well!]{.underline}

#### The possessive particle *ī* ( Y \<Y\>)

We have already seen expressions such as *man pid* "my father" and *ērān
šāh* "(the) king of the Iran(ians)." More commonly,
genitives are connected to their nouns by means of the particle *ī*,

*gyāg ī Ohrmazd ud abārīgān yazdān*

"the place of Ohrmazd and the other gods \> Ohrmazd's and the other

(beside: *Ohrmazd ud abārīgān yazdān gyāg*).

Note the common possessive expressionx *X xwēš* "belonging to X,"
literally "X's own." See more in Lesson 5.

Plural nouns take the ending *-ān* before *xwēš*, e.g.:

*kē xwēš ham* *Ahrimen ud dēwān xwēš nē ham*

"whose am I?" ("who do I belong to?") "I do not belong to Ahrimen and

### Possession 3

#### *ān ī X* "that of X"

As in the case of adjectives, one can also use the formula [*ān ī X*
"that of X"]{.underline} to express possession, e.g.:

*ān ī šāh pus*

"the king's son"

*ān ī murwān parr*

"the birds' feathers/wings"

*tan ī pasēn ān ī Ohrmazd abdom dām*

"the Final Body (is) Ohrmazd's last (*abdom*) creation"

*gāh ī rāstān ud ān ī drōzanān*

"the place of the truthful ones and that of the liars"

*ān ī ahlawān kerbag ud ān ī druwandān
wināh*

"the good deeds of the Righteous ones and the sins [of the
wicked ones]{.underline}"

*pad ān ī Ohrmazd āmurzīdārīh ud pad [ān ī
Ohrmazd]{.underline} rādīh*

"by Ohrmazd's mercy and by Ohrmazd's
generosity" (after *Bd*.26.9)

*menišn ud gōwišn ud kunišn ān ī harw dō kē weh ud kē-iz
wattar*

"thinking, speaking, and acting (are) '[both's' = belongs to
both]{.underline} (*harw dō*): (the one) who is good and also (*-iz*)
(the one) who is bad"

*ōy ān ī kē ruwān*

"whose soul (is) he (*ōy*)?" (Ardā Wirāz's question on his
trip to hell)

The construction sometimes has more pronounced deictic ('pointing')
function, "that of," which can then be con­trasted with
*ēn* (*ēd*) *ī X* "this one of X's," which usually has
pronounced deictic function (on *ōy ī*, see below), e.g.:

*mizd ī gētīy ān-iz ī mēnōy*

"the reward of (= in) this world (and) that of the other
world, too (*-iz*)"

*ēn ī man dēn ud ān ī tō kerb*

"this *dēn* of mine and that body/shape of
yours"

*ēd ī mardōmān zīndagīh*

"this people's life (this life that people
lead)"

#### *xwēš* (nÍPW \<NPŠE\>) "own, belonging to"

As we saw in Lesson 3, the pronoun *xwēš* "own" can be used in the sense
of "belonging too." Plural nouns as possessor take the ending *‑ān*. It
can be combined with *ān ī* (*ēn ī*, *ēd ī*), e.g.:

*harw gul-ēw amahrspand-ēw xwēš*

"each flower (*gul*) belongs to one *amahrspand*"

*drōš hamēmālān xwēš*

"branding (*drōš*) belongs to (his) fellow men" (*MHD*.5.1) (i.e., they
decide whether he should be branded)

*ān ī Ohrmazd xwēš*

"Ohrmazd's, that of Ohrmazd's, that which is that of =
belongs to Ohrmazd"

*ēd ī amahrspandān xwēš*

"this that belongs to the *amahrspand*s"

In the construction *ī* \... *xwēš*, *ī* is actually a relative pronoun,
but is often used for emphasis:

*pas az 10 (dah) sāl xwāstag ī man xwēš tō xwēš*

"after 10 years, the property that (is) my own (belongs to
me) (is) your own (belongs to you) (*MHD*.24.3)



#### *ōy ī*

Note also *ōy ī* used like *ān ī*. This construction can sometimes be
rendered by the English definite article e.g.:

*ōy ī mazdēsn*

"the Mazdayasnian (one/ones)"

*ōy ī druwand Ganāg Mēnōy*

"the/that wicked Foul Spirit"

*ōy ī abārōn-dād ī wēš-wināh*

"the one with evil law and greater sin"

*mihr ī šōy drōzēd ī abāg ōy ī bazakkar mard xufsēd*

"she belies (*drōzēd*) the love (*mihr*) of the husband (*šōy*) who
sleeps (*xufsēd*) with a man who-does-evil-deeds"

*ōy ī abardom rōšnān xwadāy Ohrmazd ī dādār*

"the lord (*xwadāy*) of the highest (*abardom*) lights
(*rōšnān*) (is) Ohrmazd the Creator" (*DD*.36.10)

Note the use of both *ān ī* and *ōy ī* in the following sentence:

*ruwān ka ō ham rasēnd ān ī ōy ī nēk dastwar ī ruwān-dōst
ruwān burzēnd*

"the souls, when they come together (in paradise), praise the soul of
the good *dastwar* who loves (his) soul" (*Dk*.9.20.1)

Here *ān ī* expresses possession: *ān ī dastwar
ruwān* "the soul of the *dastwar*"; *ōy ī*
is used to connect the adjective *nēk* to the noun *dastwar*: *ōy ī nēk
dastwar* "the god *dastwar*"; and *ī ruwān-dōst* is a
regular adjective construction."

### "To be" and "to have" 2

As already mentioned, the verb "to be, become" is also used to express
possession "there is an X for Y" = "Y has an X." Nouns as possessor
often take the postposition *rāy*:

*agar-it hambun-iz mihr ī amāh ast*

"if you have any love for us at all (*hambun-iz*)" (*KN*.10.3/9.8)

*Pābag rāy ēč frazend nē būd*

"Pābag had no (*ēč \... nē*) child(ren)" (after *KN*.1.5)

##### *ast* "is":

We find *ast* in certain contexts (not yet entirely defined), referring
to some preceding discussion or explanation, meaning something like
"that is (*id est*)" or "and that is X," sometimes simply "is, are"
e.g.:

*ān ast Ohrmazd*

"(Wahman said:) That (one)/*he* is Ohrmazd" (*AWN*.11.3b)

*az asrō-kerb Ahunwar frāz būd ast dēn*

"From the fire form the *Ahunwar* originated, that is, the
*dēn*" (*Bd*.1.49)

*stārag ī kōdag was hēnd kē-šān nām ēn ast*

"there are many small stars, whose (*kē-šān* 'who their') names

### The verb

#### The present indicative and the imperative

Pahlavi verbs are conjugated in three persons and two numbers (1st, 2nd,
3rd person singular and plural), as in German, French, Persian (Farsi),
etc.

The present and imperative are formed from the present stem, which
differs from the past stem in various ways (see Lesson 5). In the
Glossary, the present stem is followed by a hyphen and then the past
stem, e.g., *purs- pursīd* "ask, asked."

#### Present indicative

The endings of the present indicative are those of the verb "to be":
*ham*, *hē*, etc., except the 3rd person.

In this and subsequent lessons, we shall mainly use the 3rd person
singular and plural forms. Other forms will be used from Lesson 7 on.
Note that the present is also used to express future.

Subjects normally take no ending (*mard*, not *mardān*).



Examples:

*Zarduxšt az Ohrmazd ēdōn pursēd kū tō kē hē*

"Zarduxšt asks Ohrmazd thus: who are you?"

Note *purs-* + *az*, literally "ask (something) 'from' somebody."

*mard az mard pursēnd kū-t pad ruwān dādestān čē būd*

"men ask men: what was the judgment (*dādestān*) upon your
soul?" (after *Bd*.34.20)

Note the use of the form *mard* + plural verb *pursēnd* (not *mardān*! )

*amahrspand andar Garōdmān pēš ī Ohrmazd estēnd*

"the *amahrspand*s stand before Ohrmazd in Garōdmān"

*ahlawān kerbag ō yazdān rasēd ayāb ō dēwān*

"does the good activity (the good deeds) of the Righteous ones
come to the gods or to the demons?" (after *AWN*.1.15)

*win wāng ān ast kē ahlaw srāyēnd ī Abestāg abar
gōwēnd*

"the sound (*wāng*) of the lute (*win*) is that which the Righteous ones
sing who recite (*abar gōwēnd*) the Avesta"
(*Bd.*20)

####  Imperative 2nd person singular and plural

The imperative has no ending in the singular and in the plural is
identical with the indicative ending *-ēd*.

The negation is *ma* 'do not \...!"


Examples:

*pas Srōš-ahlaw ō man gōwēd kū ma tars*

"then Srōš-ahlaw says to me: do not fear!" (after
*AWN*.53.4)

*ō ān wināhkār zahr dahēd tā andar zamān mīrēd*

"give that sinner poison, so that (*tā*) he dies in time =
on the spot!" (*KN*.10.3/9.8)

*rāst gōwēd ān ī drōw ma gōwēd*

"Speak the truth! Do not speak
untruth/lies!"

*ay bazzakkar Ohrmazd dām ma zanēd čē ō dōšox rasēd*

"O evil-doers, do not smite Ohrmazd's creation, because
you will come to Hell!"

#### Double negations

Note the use of double negations: *nē nē* "not at all"; *nē \... (ud)
nē*, *ma \... ma*, "neither \... nor," e.g.:

*ka-š bīm ī pad margīh ēwar ud kasān-iz bīm ī pad margīh ēwar ā pādixšāy
ka nē nē kunēd*

"when fear (*bīm*; i.e. danger) of death (*margīh*) for him (*-iš*) (is)
certain and fear for the death of other people (if he does not help
them) (is) certain, then he is authorized (*pādixšāy*) not
to do it at all" (*PR*.17d.12)

*ma-iz pas-iz mazdēsn ān zamīg kārēnd ma āb
abar hilēnd kū pad ān abar sag ayāb mard widerd*

"also, afterward, let the Mazdayasnians not till that earth, let them
not release water on (it), on which (*kū pad ān abar*) a dog or a man
has passed away!" (*PV*.6.2)

Also note the use of negation in negated + positive clauses with
identical verb:

*ān mard dušmen nē bē dōst bawēd*

"that man does not become an enemy, but a
friend"

###  Reported (direct and indirect) speech

Pahlavi does not have indirect speech, like English. That is, we cannot
say "he said that they should go and tell him that he must come." This
sentence would have to be put as "he said: you go and tell him: you must
come." This is how it is said in Persian, as well.

*an nē dānam kū pad handarz-nāmag čē nibišt estēd*

"I do not know: what stands (= is) written in the will?" = "I do not
know what is written in the will" (after *REA*.7.3)

*an dādār Ohrmazd framāyam ō amahrspandān kū*

*gōwēd ō hamāg yazdān ud mēnōyān kū*

*rawēd ud rasēd ō hayārīh ī Pišišōtan ī bāmīg*

"I, Ohrmazd the creator, command the *amahrspand*s:

tell all the gods and (all others) in the other world:

go and come to the aid of Pišišōtan the bright!" (*ZWY*.7.27)

= "I, Ohrmazd the creator, command the *amahrspand*s to tell all the
gods and (all others) in the other world to go and come to the aid of
Pišišōtan the bright"

In "disjunctive" questions *kū* should be translated as "whether":

*nē dānēnd kū rāst gōwēd ayāb nē*

"they do not know: 'is he speaking the truth or not?"

= "they do not know whether he is speaking the truth or not"

##  Grammar 5

### The relative pronouns

The relative pronouns *ī* (Y \<Y\>) and *kē* (WN \<MNW\>) are both of
them common in the functions of "who, whom, which."

Note that relative clauses introduced by *ī* usually contain a verb,
which helps distinguish them from *izafe* constructions.

Examples:

*ast kē ēdōn gōwēd* *ān ī dānēd ud ān ī nē*

"there is one who says thus (as follows) \..." "the one
who knows and the one who does not"

*mardōm ī andar ēn šahr hēnd mazdēsn hēnd*

"the people who are in this town are Mazdayasnian"

*ātaxš ī Burzisawang ātaxš ī pēš ī Ohrmazd ī xwadāy estēd*

"the Burzisawang fire is the fire which stands before
Ohrmazd the Lord" (after *Bd*.18.1)

Note: The four particles in this last sentence have three different
functions. What are they?

Relative pronouns with enclitics, e.g.:

*ān ī-š andar dānišn*

"that which (is) within his knowledge"

#### "Whose"

The relative pronoun *kē* can be direct or indirect object, as well as
possessive "whose," e.g.:

*Ardaxšīr az awēšān pursīd kū ēn rēdag kē ast*

"Ardaxšīr asked them: whose (son) is this boy?" (*KN*.14.9)

*ān kē kār abāg wehān wehīh kunēd ud kē abāg
wattarān wattarīh kunēd*

"he whose business (is) with good (people) does good
things (goodness); and (he) whose (business is) with bad
ones does bad things (badness)" (after *MX*.59.12-14)

The possessive "whose" can also be expressed by attaching an enclitic
pronoun to the relative pronoun, e.g.:

*Ardawān kanīzag būd ī-š kerb was nēk*

"Ardawān had a (servant) girl, whose (lit. who her) shape
(was) very good (beautiful)"

*u-šān čē wināh būd kē-šān ruwān ōwōn garān pādifrāh
bawēd*

"and what (*čē*) sin was theirs whose soul gets (lit. who
for their soul = for whose soul there will be) such a heavy punishment?"
(after *AWN*.99.3)

The relative pronoun *čē* (nM \<ME\>) is most often used indefinitely:
"that which," *harw čē* "everything that," etc., e.g.:

*ud čē andar ān dar*

"and (that) which (is) in that chapter"

*harw čē andar dānišn ī Ohrmazd kanāragōmand*

"everything that (is) in Ohrmazd's knowledge is limited"
(after *Bd*.1.9)

Note: be careful to distinguish the various functions of *kē*, *cē*, and
*kū*:


Examples:

*ka Ardašīr ō dād ī 15-sālag rasīd āgāhīh ō Ardawān
mad*

"when Ardašīr came to (= reached) the age of 15, information came to
Ardawān" (*KN*.2.5/1.24)

*dēw gōwēnd kū amāh hagriz andar Ērānwēz nē būd hēm*

"the *dēw*s say: we have never (*hagriz \... nē*) been in Ērānwēz"

There are numerous verbal expressions made with a nominal form plus a
